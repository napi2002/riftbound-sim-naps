# AI upgrade evaluation (2026-11-16)

AI levels: **0** = original greedy card-by-card AI; **1** = + gear/unit abilities and equipping scored against cards every step, combo-enabler scoring (Karthus before Deathknell units, Equipment with Azir/Weaponmaster, early engine gear, Mighty units with Fiora/Volibear); **2** = + per-deck sequencing rules in `src/guides.json` (`ai.rules`: hold / before / prefer). Both levels also got AI values for the custom effects added for the Nov 2026 cards (before, the AI scored those spells at 0 and never cast them).

## Verdict

Steps 1–2 help where they target: Ezreal +2.2, Azir +1.7, Kennen +1.4, Jayce +0.8 points, other decks within ±0.4. They do not close the big gaps (Jayce ~20% vs 56% real, LeBlanc ~80% vs 46%): a one-move-at-a-time AI can't plan a turn. Some rules hurt and were dropped after testing (Kennen trash-fill-first −1.8, Jayce ramp-first −1.0, Sett Kingpin −1.2, LeBlanc Karthus-first rule −0.8 on top of the synergy term). The next step is turn-level lookahead (step 3).

# Step 3: turn lookahead (level 3, 2026-11-16)

Each step the AI takes its best 4 cards, 2 abilities, the legend and "stop playing", plays each on a copy of the game, finishes the turn and the opponent's reply with the level-2 AI, and scores the position (points ×12, battlefields ±3, unit value on board, cards in hand, runes and energy gear). It picks the best first move, plays it, and re-plans. **Fair play:** the copies don't see the opponent's hand or either deck order: each line redeals the opponent's hand from their unseen cards and shuffles both decks, and every candidate is averaged over 2 such guesses. (Without this the gain was +15.9 points: about half of it was from peeking.) Checks: same seed gives the same game; 73 mid-game lookahead calls left the real game state byte-identical. Browser AI turns: 60–120 ms.

# Self-play learned evaluation (level 4, 2026-11-16)

The lookahead (level 3) scores each line's end position with hand-set weights. Level 4 learns that score instead: a logistic regression of "this player wins" on 17 position features (`RT.evalFeatures`: points and points² for both, battlefields, unit value on board, Might at battlefields, units, hand, runes, energy gear, gear, Gold, buffs, XP, hidden cards, deck size, champion still in zone), fitted on self-play games (`quant/train_eval.js` → `quant/fit_eval.py`, every 5th game held out).

| Round | Self-play data | Held-out AUC (learned / hand-set) | Held-out log-loss (learned / hand-set) | Gain vs level 3 (paired, 30 games × 20 opponents per deck) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8,400 level-2 games (217,806 positions) | 0.812 / 0.759 | 0.521 / 0.574 | +3.8 points |
| 2 | + 4,200 level-4 games (327,274 positions) | 0.807 / 0.759 | 0.524 / 0.573 | +3.7 points |

Board value and closeness to 8 points matter most; runes matter more than the hand-set weights assumed; holding more battlefields at the start of your turn is slightly negative once points and board are accounted for (those points are already counted and units are spread thin).

Verdict: +3.7 points per deck over level 3 (which was +8.2 over level 2). A second self-play round didn't add more: the linear evaluation has plateaued; further gains need richer features (e.g. matchup- or deck-specific terms, threats to held battlefields) or a non-linear model. Level 4 is the game's default AI.
