AI levels: **0** = original greedy card-by-card AI; **1** = + gear/unit abilities and equipping scored against cards every step, combo-enabler scoring (Karthus before Deathknell units, Equipment with Azir/Weaponmaster, early engine gear, Mighty units with Fiora/Volibear); **2** = + per-deck sequencing rules in `src/guides.json` (`ai.rules`: hold / before / prefer). Both levels also got AI values for the custom effects added for the Nov 2026 cards (before, the AI scored those spells at 0 and never cast them).

## Verdict

Steps 1–2 help where they target: Ezreal +2.2, Azir +1.7, Kennen +1.4, Jayce +0.8 points, other decks within ±0.4. They do not close the big gaps (Jayce ~20% vs 56% real, LeBlanc ~80% vs 46%): a one-move-at-a-time AI can't plan a turn. Some rules hurt and were dropped after testing (Kennen trash-fill-first −1.8, Jayce ramp-first −1.0, Sett Kingpin −1.2, LeBlanc Karthus-first rule −0.8 on top of the synergy term). The next step is turn-level lookahead (step 3).

# Step 3: turn lookahead (level 3, 2026-11-16)

Each step the AI takes its best 4 cards, 2 abilities, the legend and "stop playing", plays each on a copy of the game, finishes the turn and the opponent's reply with the level-2 AI, and scores the position (points ×12, battlefields ±3, unit value on board, cards in hand, runes and energy gear). It picks the best first move, plays it, and re-plans. **Fair play:** the copies don't see the opponent's hand or either deck order: each line redeals the opponent's hand from their unseen cards and shuffles both decks, and every candidate is averaged over 2 such guesses. (Without this the gain was +15.9 points: about half of it was from peeking.) Checks: same seed gives the same game; 73 mid-game lookahead calls left the real game state byte-identical. Browser AI turns: 60–120 ms.

# Self-play learned evaluation (level 4, 2026-11-16)

The lookahead (level 3) scores each line's end position with hand-set weights. Level 4 learns that score instead: a logistic regression of "this player wins" on 17 position features (`RT.evalFeatures`: points and points² for both, battlefields, unit value on board, Might at battlefields, units, hand, runes, energy gear, gear, Gold, buffs, XP, hidden cards, deck size, champion still in zone), fitted on self-play games (`quant/train_eval.js` → `quant/fit_eval.py`, every 5th game held out).

## Why the bot's tier list differs from tournaments (Nov 21 2026)

**Measured bias.** Field of the 22 tournament decks at AI level 4 (20 seed pairs per matchup, 9,240 games) against real 14-day win rates to 2026-11-15: Spearman rho = -0.02 overall. The gap (sim WR minus real WR) rises with a deck's unit share (rho = +0.75) and falls with its spell share (rho = -0.61). Unit-heavy decks are overrated (Volibear +45 points, Evelynn +27, LeBlanc +24, Rengar +22, Fiora +22, Mordekaiser +22) and spell/trick decks underrated (Jayce -30, Riven -17, Ezreal -16, Kennen -12, Irelia -9).

**Cause 1: the ban confound.** The real win rates are from before the Nov 23 bans, but the sim played the post-ban lists. Re-running with the pre-ban lists (from 3f0c5b9^, plus Volibear with Dazzling Aurora and Kai'Sa with Brynhir Thundersong) at level 5, 16 seed pairs per matchup: Riven 40 -> 50%, Kennen 42 -> 54%, Jayce 32 -> 44%, Master Yi 57 -> 68%. The bans hit exactly the combo decks, so part of the "bot hates combo" effect is that the combo decks lost their key cards. Rank agreement with real data for decks with 500+ real games: rho 0.01 (post-ban lists) -> 0.34 (pre-ban lists); mean absolute error 13.9 -> 12.8 points.

**Cause 2: reactive play.** Showdown decisions (tricks, stuns, removal and Ambush during a fight) were made by a greedy one-step rule, with no lookahead. That is where spell decks win games.

**Fix: AI level 5 = showdown lookahead.** Every showdown response, and passing, is played on a copy of the game (hidden cards redealt as in level 3). The copy then finishes the showdown, the fight, the rest of the turn and the next turn at level 2 and is scored by the learned evaluation, with 2 samples per option. combat() was split so that a copied fight can resume after its showdown. Cost: +7% time per game. Level 5 vs level 4, paired (6 seed pairs, both seats, every opponent; 11,088 games):

## AI levels 6 and 7: attack search and less noisy comparisons (Nov 26 2026)

**Level 6: attack search.** Up to level 5, attacks (which units go to which battlefield, and whether to attack at all) followed a fixed rule, while card plays and showdowns already used lookahead. Level 6 builds up to 9 candidate attack plans: the rule's own plan, no attack, and for each battlefield the cheapest winning group, a group with 1-2 spare Might against tricks, everything, an empty-battlefield grab with the weakest or strongest unit, and a grab plus the best attack elsewhere. Each plan is played out on copies of the game through the rest of the turn and the opponent's reply. Every plan is scored against the same guesses of the hidden cards (6 guesses), and the bot keeps the rule's plan unless another scores clearly better. Paired A/B vs level 5 (3 seed pairs, 6,072 games): +3.8 points (3 guesses); variants: 6 guesses +4.2, switching margin 0 +4.1, 2 extra turns of horizon +3.1. 6 guesses was kept; a longer horizon didn't help.

**Level 7: same guesses for every option.** The card-play and showdown lookaheads used different random guesses of hidden cards for each option, so with 2 guesses the bot often picked whichever option got the luckiest guess. Level 7 scores every option against the same 3 guesses (common random numbers). Level 7 vs level 6: +2.4 points from the card-play change alone.

**Tried and dropped:** playing units straight onto a battlefield you control (humans do this; the engine allows it). As a fixed rule plus lookahead it cost -4.0 points, as lookahead-only -2.0, and on top of level 7's common guesses +0.4 (within noise), so it was left out.
