Pricing a card-game metagame with Monte Carlo simulation

Can a game engine with an AI player rank competitive decks the way real tournaments do, and can it forecast where the metagame moves next?

Status: engine, AI, data pipeline and harness are built; results below are in-sample (the AI was tuned while measuring). The pre-registered confirmatory tests run on tournament data collected after the engine is frozen.

1. Summary

Rift Table simulates full games of Riftbound with a heuristic AI that follows researched pro game plans for 15 tournament decks. Playing every pair of decks 2,000 times (1,000 seed pairs with seats swapped) gives each deck a win rate against the field with a 95% interval of about ±0.6 points. Against 45,836 real matches from riftbound.gg, the simulator's deck ranking has Spearman ρ = 0.52 (p = 0.047) across all decks.

The main finding so far is a piloting gap: simulated win rates spread from 14% to 81%, real ones only from 36% to 62%. Part of each simulated win rate measures how well the AI can pilot a deck, not only how strong the deck is: simple proactive plans are overrated, decks that depend on combat timing or slow engines are underrated.

4. How each deck plays

Each deck's plan was researched from tournament reports, deck techs and guides and stored as a per-turn decision tree (shown in the game's Guide). The AI uses the plan's card priorities, mulligan, reactions to hold and attack style. Highlighted decks keep 2 energy open for reactions on the opponent's turn.

Kennen
Combo / Off-tempo value · attacks: normal · keeps 1 energy · research confidence: medium

Chaos/Order combo-value: slow the opponent's scoring early while filling the trash, then swing with cheap Rhasa the Sunderer and units replayed from the trash. Playing cards from outside your hand empowers the legend, which gives a unit Assault 2 to conquer.

LeBlanc
Synergy midrange (tempo snowball) · attacks: aggro · keeps 1 energy · research confidence: medium

Mind/Order Deathknell synergy: cheap Deathknell units and Reflections die for cards, runes and free units, doubled by Karthus, Eternal. Baited Hook turns small bodies into bigger ones and snowballs the board.

Rengar
Aggro · attacks: aggro · keeps 0 energy · research confidence: high

Body/Fury aggro: win the first showdowns with cheap units, keep scoring every turn and hide real Might with Ambush. Every unit played gives +1 Might via the legend; Kai'Sa, Kinkou Initiate and Nidalee keep the cards flowing.

Irelia
Tempo / Midrange (value) · attacks: safe · keeps 2 energy · research confidence: medium

Calm/Chaos tempo-value: land Irelia, Fervent or Stellacorn Herder, then choose them with cheap tricks so the legend readies them for extra moves, draws and conquers. Cheap reactions and counters protect the one or two key units while you out-card the opponent.

Akali
Midrange (value / showdown control) · attacks: normal · keeps 2 energy · research confidence: medium

Calm/Fury value midrange: keep the board even early with Shuriken Flip and cheap units, then land Akali, Silent, Stellacorn Herder or Irelia, Fervent when they're safe. The legend pulls a unit out of a showdown on your turn, so you win fights or leave after the opponent has spent cards.

Jayce
Ramp / Combo (Aurora control) · attacks: safe · keeps 1 energy · research confidence: medium

Ramp with Platewyrm Egg, Mobilize and Catalyst, then land Dazzling Aurora. Elder Dragon is the only unit in the deck, so Aurora drops one every turn and 1-damage spells wipe boards.

Master Yi
Midrange (hold) · attacks: normal · keeps 1 energy · research confidence: high

Take a battlefield early and defend it with one unit: Yi's legend gives a lone defender +2 Might. Cheap reactions win showdowns; Rengar and Ruin Runner retake ground.

Azir
Tempo / Tokens (equipment midrange) · attacks: normal · keeps 1 energy · research confidence: high

Play one cheap Equipment a turn, then pay 1 for the legend's Sand Soldier, which equips for free. Grind with hidden removal, then Arise! and Azir - Sovereign take two battlefields at once to win.

Vex
Control (hold) · attacks: safe · keeps 2 energy · research confidence: medium

Settle Vex - Apathetic on a battlefield and hold it: units the opponent plays are stunned, hidden cards ambush attackers, and each hold draws a card. Win by out-holding, not out-fighting.

Rek'Sai
Aggro / Tempo (off-tempo tokens) · attacks: aggro · keeps 0 energy · research confidence: medium

Flood the board with 2-cost units that leave tokens behind, conquer cheaply every turn and let the legend play cards off the top. Order removal clears blockers; it wins from ahead, not behind.

Riven
Tempo / Midrange (hold-oriented) · attacks: normal · keeps 2 energy · research confidence: medium

Tempo-hold deck: take a battlefield early, then every unit you play there enters ready (legend). Ready units draw, stun, equip or pull enemies the same turn while Calm/Chaos reactions protect the board. Won Sacramento Showdown and Hartford $10K.

Evelynn
Aggro / Tempo (hidden cards) · attacks: aggro · keeps 1 energy · research confidence: low

Aggressive hidden-card deck: hide units and tricks at battlefields you hold, then flip them on your own turn to draw (legend) and swing fights. The opponent must either attack into unknown cards or let you hold for free.

Mordekaiser
Midrange (sacrifice / value) · attacks: normal · keeps 1 energy · research confidence: low

Fury/Order midrange: trade cheap units that leave value when they die, fill the trash, and banish it with the legend for extra power. Big removal units (Vi, Kai'Sa, Mordekaiser - Dominating) then win the board by force.

Orianna
Control / Midrange (gear) · attacks: safe · keeps 2 energy · research confidence: low

Calm/Mind gear control: Orianna's legend turns one gear into a unit with Might equal to its cost, so The Ball becomes a 5 Might ganker that draws or deals 2 on each move. Calm reactions protect the key gear while it holds battlefields.

Seraphine
Control (tokens) · attacks: safe · keeps 2 energy · research confidence: low

Mind/Order token control: flood the board with Recruits, then exhaust three units plus the legend to cut 2 energy off a spell. The core play is a cheap Imperial Decree followed by Bellows Breath to wipe a battlefield.

5. Hypotheses

Family-wise error is held at 5% with a Holm correction across the five primary tests. Thresholds are fixed in quant/PREREGISTRATION.md before the holdout opens.

6. Data and leakage controls

  • Time-based holdout: only matches with a window start after the engine-freeze tag are scored.
  • Point-in-time features: each weekly feature uses only snapshots fetched on or before that week; raw responses are never overwritten.
  • Overlapping windows: snapshots cover rolling 30-day windows, so tests use non-overlapping snapshots or block bootstrap.
  • Survivorship: decks that leave the snapshot stay in the panel with share 0.
  • Reproducibility: every simulated game is stored with the engine commit and its seed.

7. Method

  • Sample size from power: ±3 points per pairing at 95% needs ~1,100 games; the harness plays 2,000 per pairing in about 2.5 minutes.
  • Variance reduction: mirrored seed pairs (antithetic samples) cancel the first-player edge; common random numbers across engine versions.
  • Baselines: coin flip, Bradley–Terry, the simulator, and a hierarchical blend that pools real head-to-head data toward the simulator.
  • Robustness: H1 re-run with three AI strengths to measure how much the ranking depends on play quality.
